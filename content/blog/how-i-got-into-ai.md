In 2021 I had no background in AI. Today I build AI systems for a living. This is how that happened, and what I think mattered.

## Starting with real problems

I joined Omdena collaborative challenges, where teams from around the world work on a real problem for a few weeks. My first projects were things I didn't yet know how to do: detecting power-grid infrastructure in satellite images, and building a question-answering assistant for pig farmers. Having a concrete problem and teammates who needed my part to work taught me faster than any course.

## From contributions to contracts

Those projects were public work. People could read the code, run the demo and see how I thought. That open work led to paid contract roles, including building an AI assistant for a financial-services company.

## What I'd pass on

- **Pick a problem, not a technology.** "Find pylons in satellite images" teaches data collection, labelling, training, evaluation and deployment. "Learn deep learning" teaches vocabulary.
- **Ship the whole thing.** A model in a notebook isn't a product. Wrapping it in an API, deploying it and watching it be used is where the real lessons are.
- **Work in public.** Code and write-ups are a portfolio that keeps working while you sleep.
- **Keep studying alongside.** I've been finishing a computer science degree at Ahmadu Bello University while working, and the fundamentals pay off constantly: data structures, databases and networks show up in every AI system.

## What I'm still learning

Evaluating LLM applications properly, observing them in production, and building agentic workflows that stay reliable. Those are the problems I find myself wanting to write about next.
